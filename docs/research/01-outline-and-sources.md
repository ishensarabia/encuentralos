## 1. Findings that change the plan

1. **A new Protocolo Homologado de Búsqueda (PHB) is in force.** It was approved by the Sistema
   Nacional de Búsqueda on 2025-12-15 and published in the DOF on **2026-03-25**. It updates the
   2020 PHB to match the 2025 reform of the Ley General. We cite the 2026 version only. **[P]**
   - It **drops the term "persona no localizada"** for the five search types. Every report
     now starts an investigation file (*carpeta de investigación*). **[P]**
   - The CNB's "versión resumida para familias" (May 2025 PDF) and IDHEAS's 2019 practical guide
     are based on the **2020** PHB. Use them for plain-language style only, not as current rules.
2. **The Ley General was reformed on 2025-07-16** (last reform in the Cámara de Diputados
   consolidated text). Changes that matter to families: **[P]**
   - art. 80: *"Ningún protocolo establecerá plazos de espera para iniciar la investigación."* The
     law now bans waiting periods outright.
   - arts. 80 and 88: every report, notice or complaint means the Fiscalía **must open a carpeta
     immediately**.
   - art. 12 Duodecies: a mandatory **Ficha de Búsqueda** and **Alerta Nacional**, including
     alerts on any use of the missing person's CURP through the new Plataforma Única de Identidad.
   - art. 85 fr. I Bis: authorities ask for the CURP but **cannot make the search conditional on it**.
3. **Registry numbers are contested.** RNPDNO counts for Jalisco dropped by about 2,500 between 2025
   and 2026, and Red Lupa (IMDHD) questions whether the data is consistent. For state choice, treat
   rankings as rough indicators only. **[S]**
4. **Report without a complaint: the wording needs care.** It is still true that families do not
   need a *denuncia* for the **search** to start (art. 89; CNB can even take DNA samples "sin
   necesidad de denuncia", art. 53 fr. XXVI Sexies). But after the 2025 reform, any report
   automatically triggers an investigation by the Fiscalía. The honest message is:
   *"No necesitas poner una denuncia para que empiecen a buscar. Con tu reporte basta: la autoridad
   tiene que avisar a la Fiscalía, y la Fiscalía tiene que abrir una carpeta de investigación."*

## 4. Information and documents to gather

From PHB 2026 párr. 150 (minimum data) and LGMDFP art. 85 and art. 106 fr. II. **[P]**
The rule to show above the list: *"Lleva lo que tengas. Si te falta algo, igual te tienen que atender."*

- **Identity:** full name (and social name, if any), date of birth and age, CURP (**optional; not required**), voter ID or other ID, RFC.
- **Recent photos**, including one **smiling** (the teeth help identification).
- **Distinguishing marks:** moles, tattoos, scars, piercings, any feature, described in detail.
- **Clothing:** what they wore at last contact, and what they usually wear.
- **Last contact:** when, where, how, and with whom.
- **Phone:** number and carrier; email accounts; social media and apps (ride-hailing, maps, dating, games).
- **Routines:** home, work and school addresses, usual places, people they see.
- **Health:** medical conditions, disabilities, medication, substance use; **medical and dental history** (X-rays, dentist's name).
- **Vehicles involved:** color, plates, make, model.
- **Where you think they could be, and who could know something.**
- **Prior threats or violence**, and any similar disappearances nearby.
- **For the DNA step:** a list of blood relatives who could give samples (see §5).
- **Personal items** that could hold DNA or fingerprints.

## 5. How DNA samples work

**Plain-language summary (draft):**
*Tu ADN puede ayudar a identificar a tu familiar. Te pedirán una muestra (normalmente de saliva o
sangre) y la comparan con la de personas encontradas que no han sido identificadas. Solo se puede
usar para buscar e identificar a personas desaparecidas. Tienes que dar tu permiso por escrito.*
("saliva o sangre" is common practice but **I did not find it in the law or PHB text** [C].)

| Point | Detail | Source | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | Reference samples from relatives are compared against the Banco Nacional de Datos Forenses (BNDF), run by the FGR and connected to state forensic databases, including genetic ones. | arts. 119, 106 fr. II q–r | [P] |
| **Who should give samples** | **The whole nuclear family:** mother, father, sons, daughters, brothers, sisters. If the missing person's children donate, also take a sample from the **other parent of those children**. A family tree is recommended to spot more donors. | PHB 2026 párr. 249 d | [P] |
| Who takes them | Forensic services of the Fiscalía, and search commissions may also take, process and compare samples. The CNB runs national sampling campaigns **without a denuncia**. Relatives abroad can give samples at embassies and consulates. | art. 53 fr. XXVI Sexies; PHB 2026 párr. 109, 400 | [P] |
| **Consent** | Written consent is required before the sample is taken. | art. 123 | [P] |
| **Right to know and to a record** | The expert must explain how the information will be used and give the donor a **constancia** (record). The acta must list the missing person, each donor, their relationship, IDs and consents. | art. 122; PHB 2026 párr. 249 d | [P] |
| **Use limited to identification** | Genetic data from relatives can be used **only** to identify missing persons. | art. 108 (last párr.); art. 122 | [P] |
| Independent expert | Donors may appoint a certified independent expert, at their own cost, to be present. | art. 123 | [P] |
| Existing samples | Tell the authority if there are already samples of the person anywhere (hospital, prior tests), and keep personal items. | art. 106 fr. II r; PHB annex | [P] |
| Other identifiers | Fingerprints (AFIS), dental and medical records; ante-mortem data are cross-checked against post-mortem data. | art. 106 fr. II n; PHB 2026 párr. 249 c; IDHEAS 2025 p. 42 | [P] / [S] |

**Open questions for the reviewer:** How long do results usually take? Can families get a copy of
their profile or the comparison result? Which state labs are reliable, and should families be warned
about duplicate sampling? What is the CNIH's current role after the 2025 reform? The law is silent
on these practical points, and they are where the organization's experience matters most.

## 8. Open questions for the reviewing organization

1. Final wording of the "report vs. denuncia" message (finding 4).
2. Whether to recommend reporting to both the national and state commission (IDHEAS does), or name one first.
3. Safety guidance: what to say about sharing posters on social media, dealing with extortion calls, and joining collectives.
4. The DNA practical questions in §5.
5. Which collectives or organizations to list, and how often to recheck contacts.
6. Whether the guide should mention the amparo buscador in the first screen or later (it is powerful but intimidating).
7. Tone and terms: *"tu familiar"*, *"tu ser querido"*, or the missing person's name; inclusive language (the IDHEAS 2025 guide covers LGBTTTIQA+ specifics).
8. **Colectivos de familias.** Proposal: encourage them, in three ways.
   - **Directory:** list collectives per state, each only with its own consent (listing can put a collective at risk), vetted by the partner organization (families in crisis are targets of fake "search services" and extortion), and framed as accompaniment that complements the report to the authorities, never a replacement for it.
   - **Review:** invite collectives to review the content, alongside or as part of the reviewing organization.
   - **Co-authorship:** ask whether they want a credit or a role in the project. Who decides which collectives are listed, and how often that list is rechecked?
